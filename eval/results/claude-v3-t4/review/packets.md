case_id: pinnacle_licensee
Question: What happens to Pinnacle's exclusive technology license and fees after a change of control of the licensee, and can either party terminate?
Reviewer notes: Conversion is automatic absent prior written consent to retain exclusivity. Payment duties persist; the licensee control change itself confers no termination right.
Candidate answer: On a Change of Control of the Licensee (acquisition of more than 50% of voting securities), the exclusive license automatically converts to non-exclusive on the consummation date unless Pinnacle (Licensor) gives prior written consent to keep exclusivity, which it may withhold in its sole discretion. The Annual License Fee is unchanged (as escalated) and remains fully payable for the rest of the Term. Neither party gets a termination right from the Change of Control itself. Termination remains available only on independent grounds such as uncured material breach, insolvency, or infringement. Failure to give timely notice is itself a material breach, which could support termination under 12.3 after the 60-day cure period.

case_id: apex_msa_successor
Question: Does the permitted assignment to a merger successor in Apex's master services agreement eliminate the customer's change-of-control termination right? State the exercise and notice periods.
Reviewer notes: The assignment exception is subject to 12.3: customer may give 60-day notice within 120 days of receiving provider's written control-change notice. Provider must notify within ten business days after consummation.
Candidate answer: No. The permitted assignment to a merger successor in Section 16.1 does not eliminate the Customer's change-of-control termination right; 16.1 is expressly subject to, and does not limit or modify, Customer's rights under Section 12.3. The right arises only if the transaction is a Change of Control of Provider as defined (more than 50% change in voting equity, or sale of all or substantially all assets). Exercise: Customer may terminate on 60 days' prior written notice to Provider, exercisable within 120 days after Customer receives Provider's written notice of the Change of Control. Provider must give that notice within 10 business days after consummation. If Customer does not give termination notice within the 120 days, it is deemed to have waived the right for that event only.

Question: Does TerraVerde's lease treat a transfer of more than 50% of the tenant as an assignment, and what consent and landlord recapture rights apply?
Reviewer notes: Section 22 treats control transfers as assignments. Landlord may elect recapture within 30 days of notice with termination 180 days after exercise; otherwise inspect consent conditions. Parser stores subclauses together under sections 21 and 22.

packet_id: P07
case_id: terranode_notice_affiliate
Question: For TerraNode's infrastructure agreement, what consent and advance notice are needed for a change of control versus an assignment to an affiliate, and does silence mean consent?
Reviewer notes: General control transactions: 45-day notice and 30-day response, silence is not consent. Affiliate assignment has a conditional exception including 15-day notice, written assumption and continuing liability.
Candidate answer: Change of control: under Section 9.1 a change of control (defined to include any transaction changing ultimate controlling ownership or management authority) is treated as an assignment needing the other Party's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed. Under 9.2 consent may reasonably be withheld if the acquirer (or any of its Affiliates) is a Direct Competitor of Provider (more than 15% of annual gross revenue from cloud infrastructure services). Section 9.4 requires written notice at least 45 days before the anticipated closing, with a description of the deal, closing date and acquirer identity (including Affiliates in cloud infrastructure); the other Party must respond within 30 days of a complete notice. Assignment to an Affiliate: under 9.5 no consent is needed, but the assigning Party must give at least 15 days' prior written notice, the assignee must assume all obligations in writing, and the assignor stays jointly and severally liable. Ceasing to be an Affiliate later is a new assignment requiring 9.1 consent. Silence does not mean consent: 9.4 says failure to respond within 30 days is not deemed consent. Unauthorized assignment is void (9.1) and permits termination on 30 days' notice, curable by obtaining consent within 60 days (5.5, 9.3). One tension: 9.4's 45-day notice applies to any transaction 'that would constitute an assignment under this Article 9', which on its face could also cover Affiliate assignments, though 9.5 sets its own 15-day period; the text does not reconcile these.

case_id: terranode_competitor
Question: Does TerraNode have termination rights if Aldersgate is acquired by a competitor?
Reviewer notes: Competitor status requires the revenue definition and affiliate analysis. Acquisition alone is not an unconditional termination right: check consent breach, written notice and opportunity to cure. Section 9.3 is an alternative source of the cure rule, not a mandatory duplicate of 5.5.
Candidate answer: Possibly, but not automatically. Under the TerraNode ISA, an acquisition of Aldersgate is treated as an assignment (change of control) needing TerraNode's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld). TerraNode may reasonably withhold consent if the acquirer or any Affiliate is a Direct Competitor, meaning it derives more than 15% of annual gross revenue from cloud infrastructure services. If the acquisition closes without the required consent, TerraNode may terminate on 30 days' written notice, but termination does not take effect if Aldersgate obtains consent within 60 days after receiving the termination notice. Being a 'competitor' in a general sense is not enough; the contractual Direct Competitor test controls, and no termination right arises merely from the acquirer's identity, only from closing without required consent.

case_id: wellstone_options
Question: Under Wellstone's manufacturing agreement, what termination or renegotiation options and notice deadlines apply after a change of control?
Reviewer notes: 60-day termination notice within 90 days of control change; alternatively 60-day renegotiation followed by 30-day termination notice on failure. Transaction notice is due within 15 days of announcement/signing.
Candidate answer: Yes, conditionally. If either Wellstone or Solara undergoes a Change of Control (transfer of more than 50% of voting equity to a single acquirer or related acquirers acting in concert), the other (non-affected) party has two options. (1) Terminate under §11.1 on 60 days' written notice, delivered within 90 days of the effective date of the Change of Control. (2) Under §11.2, elect in lieu of termination to renegotiate pricing and volume commitments in good faith for 60 days following notice of the Change of Control; if no agreement is reached in that period, it may thereafter terminate on 30 days' written notice. Separately, §11.3 requires each party to give written notice of an anticipated Change of Control no later than 15 days after public announcement or signing of a definitive agreement, including a description of the transaction, the acquirer's identity and the anticipated closing date, to the extent not restricted by confidentiality. The right exists only if the transaction meets the definition and the party exercising is the non-affected party. The investigator's note that §11.1 has no cure period is consistent with the text I see, but the absence of fees or transition assistance can only be said as to the retrieved sections.
